Introduction

Recent audio-text large language models can follow natural language prompts, describe music, and answer music-related questions. However, many existing evaluations remain narrow: they focus on a small set of music tasks, convert answers into multiple-choice formats, or use ad-hoc text metrics that do not match established Music Information Retrieval (MIR) practice.

This project provides a benchmark-centered view of whether audio LLMs can solve the kinds of MIR problems that music researchers have studied for decades. The benchmark turns traditional MIR annotations into instruction-following prompts while keeping the original task-specific metrics. As a result, audio LLMs can be compared not only with each other, but also against specialized supervised MIR systems.

The benchmark is designed around the observation that music understanding is not a single ability. A model may describe a song fluently while still failing to estimate pitch, align beats, transcribe lyrics, identify culturally specific forms, or produce a valid list of timestamped events. The evaluation therefore separates high-level semantic tasks from fine-grained, continuous, and sequential MIR tasks.

Core idea: The benchmark bridges natural-language interaction and MIR evaluation. Models receive audio plus an instruction, but their answers are judged with metrics appropriate for the underlying music task.

Benchmark Composition

The benchmark covers 17 MIR task types across 29 datasets. Each dataset is converted into an audio-conditioned instruction-following format, while evaluation remains tied to the original MIR task and metric.

Evaluation Protocol

Models produce open-ended answers, which are normalized into task-specific formats and scored with established MIR/NLP metrics rather than multiple-choice accuracy.

Open-ended Outputs

Labels, scalar scores, captions, lyrics, timestamps, tuples, and segment intervals are parsed from free-form model responses.

Task-specific Metrics

Evaluation uses metrics such as ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, R2, F-measure, melody accuracy, WER, CER, BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, and BERTScore.

Cost-aware API Sampling

For proprietary models, a 11,289-example subset preserves full-test rankings using Kendall's τ and Spearman's ρ thresholds.

Results and Discussion

The results show that current audio LLMs are promising on some semantic music tasks, but they are not yet reliable replacements for specialized MIR systems.

01

Specialized MIR baselines still lead. Most evaluated audio LLMs remain behind task-specific supervised systems on standard MIR metrics, especially for note pitch, melody extraction, beat/downbeat tracking, and fine-grained classification.

02

Semantic tasks are the relative bright spot. Some models are competitive on music captioning and GTZAN genre classification, showing that high-level music semantics are closer to current model strengths.

03

Training-set alignment matters. Peak performance often appears on datasets or task families that are likely close to a model's training distribution, revealing limited generalization to unseen MIR settings.

04

Sequential MIR remains a bottleneck. Dense timestamped outputs, interval boundaries, and tuple formats are brittle for current instruction-following audio models.
